## The Five-Filter Framework (SEEOS)

This checker uses the **SEEOS** framework to evaluate AI claims:

| Letter | Filter | Question it answers |
|--------|--------|---------------------|
| **S** | Source Incentives | Who benefits if you believe this? What are they selling? |
| **E** | Evidence Shown | What concrete artifacts back the claim? What's conspicuously missing? |
| **E** | (other) Explanations | What else could explain the result besides the stated cause? |
| **O** | (reasoning h)Olds | Does the logic chain from evidence to conclusion actually hold? |
| **S** | Survives Deployment | Would this claim hold up in real-world conditions, not just benchmarks? |

---

## How the filters work

Each filter is scored on a 0–4 dial:

- **0** — No signal; cannot evaluate
- **1** — Red flag; claim fails this filter
- **2** — Weak; significant concerns
- **3** — Acceptable; minor concerns
- **4** — Strong; filter passed cleanly

The **weakest filter** determines the overall credibility ceiling. A claim cannot be more credible than its lowest dial.

---

## Using SEEOS

1. **Pin the claim** — Get the exact wording, source, and stakes
2. **Run all five filters** — Score each 0–4 with notes
3. **Identify the weakest** — This is your deciding filter
4. **Call the verdict** — State your position and the cost of being wrong
5. **Name the flip** — What evidence would change your read, and by when

---

## Why this order

Source incentives come first because they frame everything else. A neutral source with weak evidence reads differently than a vendor with weak evidence.

Evidence shown comes before reasoning holds because you can't evaluate logic without knowing what's actually on the table.

Other explanations force you to steelman alternatives before committing.

Survives deployment is last because it's the reality check — benchmarks aren't production.
